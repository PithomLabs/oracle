**Objective:** Investigate whether existing mathematical machinery connecting adelic harmonic analysis (Tate-Iwasawa theory) to spectral/dynamical-systems constructions — particularly work arising from the Hilbert-Pólya conjecture and the Berry-Keating and Connes programs on the Riemann Hypothesis — offers concrete, reusable tools for a specific open problem: constructing a linear, self-adjoint spectral generator (a "quantum-amplitude-layer" candidate) emerging naturally from deterministic dynamics on an adelic configuration space. This is background research for a theoretical-physics program combining Bohmian mechanics with a number-theoretic invariant-set substrate; the goal is to determine what concrete machinery exists, not to argue RH or BM-IST are related in substance.

**Background context to orient the search (established, not to be re-derived):**
- Tate's thesis (1950) and Iwasawa's independent work reformulate the analytic continuation and functional equation of $\zeta(s)$ using Fourier/harmonic analysis on the adele group $\mathbb{A}_\mathbb{Q}$, exploiting the adeles' self-duality (Pontryagin duality) and Poisson summation.
- The Hilbert-Pólya conjecture proposes the nontrivial zeros of $\zeta(s)$ are eigenvalues of some self-adjoint operator, making RH a spectral statement; the Berry-Keating program has searched for such an operator (e.g., a regularized $xp$-type operator) with partial, inconclusive numerical support via random-matrix statistics.
- Alain Connes' 1999 trace-formula approach realizes the zeros spectrally via the adele class space $\mathbb{A}_\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Q}^\times$, using an explicit trace formula rather than a directly-diagonalized operator.
- The physics-side open problem this feeds: whether deterministic dynamics on a p-adic/adelic invariant set can produce a closed, linear, second-order-generator observable subspace (via Koopman operator theory), with particular interest in whether **singular continuous spectrum** — associated with fractal spectral measures, unlike pure-point or absolutely-continuous spectrum — is the natural home for such a construction.

**Research questions:**

1. **Tate-Iwasawa harmonic analysis beyond the static case.** Has Tate/Iwasawa-style harmonic analysis on $\mathbb{A}_\mathbb{Q}$ (or adele groups more generally) been extended to genuinely *dynamical* settings — i.e., time-evolving or iterated systems on adelic spaces, rather than the static Fourier-analytic construction used for the zeta functional equation? Distinguish this from the already-surveyed algebraic/homogeneous adelic dynamics (Schmidt's solenoids, Einsiedler-Katok-Lindenstrauss rigidity) — the specific question is whether Tate's *self-duality* property specifically has been exploited in a dynamical or spectral-operator construction.

2. **Connes' adele class space program — structural details.** Survey the Connes 1999 trace-formula construction and its major follow-ups (Connes-Consani, Connes-Marcolli, and any recent 2020s work) in enough technical detail to answer: does the construction produce (or come close to producing) a genuinely self-adjoint linear operator with a discrete/quantized spectrum, or does it remain fundamentally at the level of a trace formula (a spectral *statement* rather than a spectral *operator*)? What role does the idele class group's structure (as opposed to a bare adele group) play, and does it matter for whether this generalizes beyond $\mathbb{Q}$?

3. **Berry-Keating and quantum-chaos candidate operators — adelic or p-adic variants.** Has any proposed Hilbert-Pólya candidate operator (Berry-Keating's $xp$, or successors) been constructed on, or reformulated over, a p-adic or adelic configuration space, as opposed to purely real/complex phase space? Include any p-adic quantum chaos literature (p-adic analogues of quantum ergodicity, p-adic random matrix theory) that might supply relevant technical tools even without direct RH content.

4. **Singular continuous spectrum in this literature.** Does any of the above — Tate-Iwasawa dynamics, Connes' construction, Berry-Keating-style operators, or p-adic quantum chaos — exhibit, require, or discuss singular continuous spectral type specifically? Separately, survey general mathematical-physics literature (outside the RH context) connecting singular continuous spectrum to fractal or self-similar/adelic structures, to assess whether this spectral regime is a natural, well-precedented target or a genuinely under-explored one.

5. **Direct search for existing syntheses.** Search specifically for literature explicitly combining any two of: "adelic dynamics," "Tate's thesis," "Koopman operator," "quantum chaos," "Hilbert-Pólya," "Berry-Keating," "p-adic quantum mechanics." Report explicitly where no such combination exists in the literature — a negative result (this specific combination is unstudied) is a valid and useful outcome, and should not be stretched into a false positive.

6. **Reusable machinery independent of RH's truth.** Given that RH itself is irrelevant to the physics question, identify whether any *technique* from questions 1–4 — regularization methods, trace-formula construction techniques, self-duality exploitation, p-adic operator constructions — could plausibly be adapted to construct a linear spectral generator from an unrelated deterministic dynamical system, independent of whether that technique has ever been used for anything RH-related.

**Exclusion criteria:** Exclude popular-science coverage of the Riemann Hypothesis or of any recent Navier-Stokes/Millennium Prize news; exclude the large body of non-peer-reviewed amateur "RH proof" claims circulating online (a well-known phenomenon in this specific area — treat any claimed proof or resolution of RH found during this search with default skepticism and flag it explicitly rather than reporting it as established); exclude numerology or "sacred geometry"-style content connecting spirals, vortices, or wave motifs to mathematics without a specific operator or equation; prioritize peer-reviewed mathematics/mathematical-physics literature (journals, arXiv math.NT/math-ph/nlin.CD preprints) over blog posts, except where a specific named technical result (e.g., a specific Connes-Consani paper) is best documented informally.

**Deliverable format:** For each of the six research questions, provide: (a) the most relevant 3–5 papers/results with full citations, (b) a plain-language summary of what each actually establishes, (c) an explicit note on whether it's a static/structural result or a genuinely dynamical/operator-theoretic one, and (d) an explicit final verdict on question 6: is there any concrete, citable technique here that could inform BM-IST's Gate 3 search, or does the honest state remain "same category of mathematical object, no working bridge yet constructed by anyone"?
