## Part 1 — For the Non-Technical Reader

### The Question Nobody Else Is Asking

Imagine you want to build a house. You have to start with a foundation. Some builders choose concrete. Some choose steel. Some choose wood. The choice of foundation determines everything that comes after — what walls you can build, what roof you can support, what weather you can withstand.

Quantum gravity has the same problem. Every theory has to choose a foundation — a "bottom layer" from which everything else emerges. And almost every theory chooses one of three materials:

- **Geometry:** Space is made of tiny loops or triangles or causal connections.
- **Computation:** The universe is a giant computer program running on a grid of bits.
- **Algebra:** Reality is made of abstract mathematical structures like strings or matrices.

The tripartite program chooses a fourth material: **arithmetic** — the mathematics of numbers.

This is the one choice no other quantum gravity theory makes. It is the program's distinctive wager. And the question is: why bet on arithmetic and nothing else?

### The River and the Bed

Think of a river. Water flows only where the riverbed has been carved. The bed determines where the water can go.

In the tripartite program, the riverbed is called the **invariant set**. It is the set of states that are physically realizable. Most conceivable states are not on it — they are like water trying to flow uphill. Only the states on the invariant set are "downhill" from the dynamics.

The invariant set is not a grid or a lattice. It is a **fractal** — a shape with structure at every scale. And its structure is determined by number theory: specifically, by the properties of numbers in a hierarchical, p-adic arrangement.

This is what "arithmetic substrate" means. The bottom layer of reality is not geometry or computation. It is number theory.

### Why Arithmetic? The Five Reasons

There are five reasons the program bets on arithmetic. Each is a concrete advantage that no other substrate has.

**1. Discreteness is intrinsic.**

In geometry or computation, you have to **impose** discreteness. You have to say "space is made of tiny triangles" or "time ticks in discrete steps." That is an extra assumption.

In arithmetic, discreteness is already there. The integers are discrete by definition. You do not need to add a grid; the discreteness is built into the mathematics from the start.

**2. The measure is natural.**

The program needs a way to assign probabilities to different states — a "measure" that tells you how likely each configuration is.

In arithmetic, there is a natural measure called the **Haar measure**. It is the unique measure that respects the arithmetic structure. You do not have to invent it; it is already there.

No other substrate has a natural measure. Geometry has no preferred measure. Computation has no preferred measure. Only arithmetic does.

**3. The hierarchy is natural.**

The program needs a way to "zoom out" — to coarse-grain the substrate and produce effective descriptions at larger scales.

In arithmetic, the p-adic metric provides a natural hierarchy. Numbers are "close" if they share a lot of structure, like postal codes. This hierarchy is exactly what the coarse-graining flow needs.

No other substrate has a natural hierarchy. Geometry is flat. Computation is flat. Only arithmetic is hierarchical.

**4. The phase is natural.**

Quantum mechanics needs a complex phase — the thing that produces interference patterns. The program needs a way to generate this phase from the substrate.

In arithmetic, the **additive characters** — `χ_p(x) = e^(2πi{x}_p)` — are the natural source of unit-circle phases. They emerge from the p-adic structure. You do not have to invent them.

No other substrate has a natural phase structure. Geometry has no phase. Computation has no phase. Only arithmetic does.

**5. The complex amplitude is natural.**

Quantum mechanics needs a complex amplitude — the wave function. The program needs a way to generate this amplitude from the substrate.

In arithmetic, the **tame/Kronecker factor** is the natural structure that supports the phase. It emerges from the arithmetic dynamics. You do not have to invent it.

No other substrate has a natural amplitude structure. Geometry has no amplitude. Computation has no amplitude. Only arithmetic does.

### The Gate That No One Else Has Even Opened

The attached survey of sixteen quantum gravity theories found something striking. Every theory has a donor for almost every problem — a technique, a precedent, a failure map. But there is **one gate where no theory has anything**: the amplitude gate.

The amplitude gate is the question of how a discrete substrate produces a complex amplitude sector — the wave function, the phase, the interference.

No theory in the entire survey has even opened this question. Every other discrete-substrate theory either assumes a Hilbert space from the start, or never needs a phase sector at all.

This is the program's unique burden. It is the one thing no one else is working on. And it is the program's strongest bet: if arithmetic can produce the amplitude, it will be the first substrate to do so.

### The Main Thesis, Stated Plainly

The program's thesis is not "BM, IST, and AS are correct, and the rest of quantum gravity confirms it."

It is narrower and more defensible:

> **Every serious rival research program has independently run into the same handful of walls — measure existence, the projection problem, the amplitude gate. This program's specific, falsifiable claim is that an arithmetic substrate is the one that gets furthest past them.**

The survey of quantum gravity theories does not prove this claim. It shows the claim is not obviously wrong. It also shows exactly which walls are shared — measure existence, the projection problem — and which one belongs to this program alone: the amplitude gate.

That is the bet. It is not a proof. But it is a bet with reasons.

## Part 2 — For the Technical Reader

### The Four Load-Bearing Claims

The tripartite program's distinctive thesis is not a package of three theories. It is a set of four claims, each of which no rival program shares.

#### Claim 1 — Determinism at the Bottom

Every surveyed rival except the Wolfram-model family is stochastic or sum-over-histories at the fundamental level:

- LQG: canonical quantization with a Hilbert-space path integral.
- CDT: sum over triangulations with a Wick rotation.
- Causal sets: stochastic sequential growth.
- String theory: perturbative sum over worldsheets.
- Euclidean QG: functional integral over metrics.
- GFT: path integral over group fields.

The tripartite program commits to a **deterministic microscopic update** `F: X → X`. This is not a small commitment. The causal-set and CDT literature on Lorentz invariance suggests that deterministic dynamics may be the **harder** branch to reconcile with relativity, not the easier one. A deterministic `F` lacks the stochastic machinery that these theories use to preserve Lorentz invariance.

**The burden:** construct a deterministic `F` whose coarse-graining produces a Lorentz-invariant IR effective theory. No donor supplies this.

#### Claim 2 — Arithmetic as the Incumbent Substrate

No rival theory in the entire survey places number theory at the bottom:

- LQG and causal sets: geometric.
- Wolfram and 't Hooft: computational.
- String and matrix models: algebraic, but not arithmetic in the specific p-adic/adelic/height/Markov sense.

The arithmetic layer is the program's distinctive, unshared wager. It is chosen because arithmetic supplies five things no other substrate supplies:

1. **Intrinsic discreteness** — the integers are discrete without imposition.
2. **Natural measure** — the Haar measure on `ℚ_p`.
3. **Natural hierarchy** — the p-adic ultrametric.
4. **Natural phase** — the additive characters `χ_p(x)`.
5. **Natural amplitude factor** — the tame/Kronecker structure.

**The burden:** construct the arithmetic substrate `(X, F)` explicitly and demonstrate that it satisfies the joint requirements (canonical height + Markov partition) on the same map.

#### Claim 3 — Born Statistics from the Substrate

The Born rule is a postulate in standard QM. It is a statistical equilibrium in Bohmian mechanics. But **no theory in the survey derives `|ψ|²` from a discrete deterministic dynamics**.

- Standard BM: typicality arguments, not derivation.
- Causal sets: growth-law measures, not Born.
- 't Hooft CAI: Born statistics via information loss/gauge equivalence, but with hand-picked structures per model.
- Wolfram: no explicit Born derivation.

The tripartite program's Gates G3 (amplitude-factor existence) and G6 (Born identification) together are **unwon everywhere**, not just here.

**The burden:** derive `Π_IR# μ_* = |ψ|²` from the substrate's invariant measure, with a stated resolution scale for any residual texture.

#### Claim 4 — The Reconciliation Itself

Every donor theory, honestly imported, becomes exactly one of four things:

1. **A precedent** — an existence proof that some version of the chain can work (e.g., Causal Fermion Systems for the discrete-to-continuum typing discipline).
2. **A template** — a technique for a specific named gate (e.g., Reginatto/Hall–Reginatto for the Fisher-information derivation of Schrödinger dynamics).
3. **A constraint** — a failure map of traps to avoid (e.g., Hořava–Lifshitz for the crossover problem).
4. **A null model** — a cheaper rival to be tested against (e.g., standard BM, non-arithmetic discrete substrates).

None of them becomes a fifth theory added to the stack. This discipline — **rejecting theory collage** — is what keeps the synthesis from collapsing into an unprincipled aggregation.

**The burden:** apply this discipline to every donor theory. Borrow the technique, precedent, or constraint. Do not borrow the ontology.

### Why Arithmetic and Nothing Else: The Technical Answer

The arithmetic commitment is a research bet, not a protected conclusion. It is retained because the incumbent substrate must simultaneously support:

1. **Discreteness** without importing a continuum Hilbert space.
2. **A nontrivial invariant-measure problem** posed with existing mathematics.
3. **Scale-sensitive coarse-graining** with a natural hierarchy.
4. **An ultrametric/place structure** that supports additive characters.
5. **A tame/Kronecker factor** that can carry the complex amplitude.

Arithmetic dynamics is the only hypothesis class in the survey that supplies all five. Geometry lacks (2) and (4). Computation lacks (4) and (5). Algebra lacks (1) and (2).

The program uses a **finite null budget** rather than an unlimited search. The initial classes are:

1. A globally algebraic degree-2 map with an explicit `p=2` local realization.
2. The analogous `p=3` class.
3. A multi-place `p=2×3` construction if single-place structure fails.
4. A non-arithmetic symbolic/graph-dynamical control of comparable finite complexity.

Any further class requires an explicit pre-registered reason. The arithmetic layer is abandoned if it cannot outperform this finite null budget by producing a distinctive, non-retrofitted bridge or prediction.

The survey reveals a convergence that is itself evidence:

- **Measure existence:** every discrete QG program stalls here (G4).
- **Projection to observables:** every discrete QG program stalls here (G5).
- **Amplitude/phase sector:** no discrete QG program has even opened this (G3).

The first two walls are shared. The third is unique. The program's design places the gates in the order it does **because** the shared walls are where every rival program has failed, and the unique wall is where no rival has even tried.

This is not proof. It is the shape of a bet that is worth making.

### What Would Count as Progress

The program's own design rule is: **optimal design is reached when no remaining feature can be removed without losing a load-bearing constraint, prediction, or falsifiability condition.**

Progress is not measured by adding more theories. It is measured by:

1. **Removing assumptions** — every assumption that can be derived is removed from the primitive core.
2. **Closing gates** — G0 through G10 are the program's own checklist.
3. **Beating null models** — the pre-registered rivals must be tested against, and ideally beaten.
4. **Triggering kill conditions** — the program is designed to fail cleanly when it fails.

A clean no-go is a successful research outcome. The program's goal is not to prove it is right. It is to find out exactly where it breaks.
