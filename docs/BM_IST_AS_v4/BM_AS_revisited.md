## Part 2: Asymptotic Safety, revisited from first principles

**§4.1 — Name the contradiction plainly: AS and IST start from literally opposite founding axioms, and the earlier material papered over this rather than resolving it.** AS's central definitional claim is that spacetime remains a well-defined continuum quantum field *at every scale*, including trans-Planckian — that's what "asymptotically safe" means: no breakdown, no discreteness, just a scale-invariant fixed point of a continuum theory. IST's central claim is the opposite: finite information capacity, no continuum at the fundamental level, period. The AS-template material two rounds ago glossed this with "the p-adic metric is the UV metric; the Euclidean metric is the IR metric" — but AS's own notion of "UV" *still means continuum field theory, just at short distance*. That sentence doesn't resolve the contradiction; it relabels it. This needs to be stated honestly as the single largest coherence risk in the whole tripartite synthesis, not smoothed over with a UV/IR-sounding phrase.

**§4.2 — The conservative, defensible fix: narrow AS's scope back to what it actually is a theory of.** AS, in the literature, is a proposal about the *gravitational/metric sector specifically* — matter is normally additional fields coupled to a dynamical background, not something AS derives on its own. The coherent move is to **stop claiming AS's RG flow and IST's arithmetic dynamics are the same object**, and instead let AS govern gravity's own coupling to whatever coarse-grained stress-energy the (eventually emergent) matter content of the IST substrate produces. This is a real scope reduction — it gives up the more ambitious "one flow generates both" framing from two rounds ago — but it doesn't require AS to explain something it was never built to explain, and it removes the continuum/discrete contradiction by simply not asking AS to be fundamental at the same layer IST claims to be.

**§4.3 — The higher-risk alternative, flagged honestly as secondary: treat AS's own continuum as itself emergent.** There's real precedent for a continuum metric field being a coarse-grained, thermodynamic-style consequence of an underlying discrete/statistical structure — Jacobson's derivation of the Einstein equations from horizon thermodynamics, and Verlinde's entropic-gravity program, are the citable examples. If this route is taken, AS's own NGFP would not be truly fundamental either — it would be a downstream, emergent feature of the discrete substrate's coarse-graining, on the same footing IST's own structures are already being demoted to. This is more ambitious, requires its own long derivation chain that doesn't exist yet, and should not be adopted as the working assumption without flagging it as exactly that — an option, not a result.

**§4.4 — The NGFP's own existence is scheme-dependent and contested within AS's own community; don't treat it as a fixed target.** This was already correctly identified as a live concern in the second BM-IA file reviewed two turns ago (its Group F, "scheme-independence gate": every derived constant must be shown invariant under regulator scheme and truncation order, or it's a gauge artifact, not a prediction). That guardrail is good and should be adopted here explicitly, not left sitting in a separate lineage's document — any claim in this architecture that leans on "the AS fixed point" needs to specify which truncation it's using and hasn't yet checked scheme-independence.

**§4.5 — Background-field dependence in the FRG calculation is a live technical gap, and §4.3 (if adopted) would actually explain it rather than needing to apologize for it.** FRG computations typically require a background metric to define the regulator, and showing this doesn't leak into physical predictions is a known, only partially resolved problem in AS. If AS's continuum is emergent (§4.3) rather than fundamental, residual background-dependence in an approximate calculation of an approximate/emergent object is expected, not alarming — but this is a consistency point in favor of §4.3, not evidence for it; the calculational problem itself remains open either way.

**§4.6 — BM's demands make AS's Euclidean/Lorentzian gap newly urgent, and this is a genuine two-way bridge rather than a one-sided import.** AS's fixed-point evidence is mostly computed in Euclidean signature; whether it survives correctly continued to Lorentzian signature, with real-time unitary evolution intact, is an under-addressed question in AS's own literature. BM, by contrast, is explicitly a real-time, deterministic, unitary theory with no collapse — if BM's trajectories are meant to live on top of whatever AS ultimately supplies, AS's Euclidean/Lorentzian gap stops being an internal AS nicety and becomes a hard prerequisite for BM to even be well-defined in this architecture. That's worth stating as a concrete, specific demand this synthesis places on AS — not previously visible from within AS's own literature alone, since AS doesn't usually need to host a fully deterministic real-time trajectory theory on top of it.

**§4.7 — AS-with-matter is a live research question, not a settled input.** Whether a realistic matter content is compatible with (or required for) the NGFP's existence is actively studied and unresolved in the AS literature itself. This means IST's substrate, whatever it turns out to produce as an emergent matter sector, isn't automatically guaranteed to be compatible with AS's own gravitational fixed point — that compatibility is a real, currently unaddressed dependency, not something safe to assume will just work out.
