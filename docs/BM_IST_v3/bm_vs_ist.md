Is IST Basically a Loose Reformulation of Bohmian Mechanics?
No, IST is not a loose reformulation of Bohmian Mechanics; it is a radical mathematical and ontological divergence. 
While Palmer explicitly acknowledges the philosophical debt IST owes to Bohm and Hiley’s concept of the "Undivided Universe," he frames IST as a necessary correction to BM’s most glaring flaw: explicit nonlocality. Palmer notes that past attempts to geometrize BM (such as Bryce DeWitt’s) relied on Riemannian geometry (the continuum). IST, by contrast, relies on p-adic fractal geometry (the discrete). Palmer explicitly states that a "final convergence" between the two theories "lies in the future" and requires modifying BM to eschew the continuum. Therefore, IST is currently a parallel, competing framework that attempts to achieve BM's philosophical goals (determinism and realism) using an entirely different mathematical engine.
Here is the detailed breakdown organized by category.
1. Fundamental Assumptions
Bohmian Mechanics (BM)

    The Continuum: Assumes the standard continuous Hilbert space over real and complex numbers.
    The Quantum Potential: Assumes the existence of a continuous, nonlocal "quantum potential" that guides particle trajectories.
    Nonlocality: Assumes that the universe is fundamentally nonlocal (EPR/Bell nonlocality) to account for entanglement and the violation of Bell's inequalities.
    Counterfactual Definiteness (CD): Assumes that it is physically meaningful to ask what the outcome of a measurement would have been if a different measurement setting had been chosen.

Invariant Set Theory / Rational Quantum Mechanics (IST/RaQM)

    The Invariant Set Postulate: Assumes the universe is a deterministic chaotic system evolving precisely on a fractal invariant set in state space.

    The p-adic Metric: Assumes that the distance between different worlds in state space (e.g., the real world vs. a counterfactual world) must be measured using the non-classical p-adic metric, not the standard Euclidean metric.
    Machian Holism: Assumes the laws of physics are defined geometrically in state space and directly determine both large-scale and small-scale structure (holism), rather than large-scale structure emerging from small-scale local laws.

2. Core Similarities (The Philosophical DNA)

    The "Undivided Universe": Both embrace a deeply holistic view of reality. BM achieves this via the nonlocal quantum potential connecting all particles. IST achieves this via Mach’s Principle and the global, fractal geometry of the invariant set, where "inertia here is due to mass there."

3. Fundamental Differences (The Mathematical and Physical Divergence)
A. Resolution of Bell’s Theorem (Locality vs. Counterfactuals)

    BM: Saves Counterfactual Definiteness but sacrifices Local Causality. It accepts "spooky action at a distance" as a real, physical phenomenon.
    IST: Saves Local Causality but sacrifices Counterfactual Definiteness (CD). IST argues that Bell's inequality is violated not because of nonlocal signaling, but because the "counterfactual" measurement settings required to derive the inequality involve irrational numbers. Since irrational states lie off the fractal invariant set, they are mathematically undefined and physically impossible. Thus, IST is locally causal.

B. The Treatment of Measurement Independence (MI) and "Conspiracy"

    BM: Often faces the accusation that any local hidden variable theory must be "superdeterministic" in a grotesquely conspiratorial way, rigging the experimenters' choices to match the particles' states.
    IST: Solves the conspiracy objection by distinguishing between nominal and exact measurement settings. An experimenter is free to choose a nominal setting (e.g., setting an interferometer arm to 0). However, they have no control over the exact setting due to unavoidable physical noise, such as a passing gravitational wave from a distant galaxy. Because exact settings must satisfy rationality conditions to lie on the invariant set, MI is violated for exact settings, but in a natural, non-conspiratorial way dictated by gravity and chaos theory.

C. Computability and Classicality

    BM: Is essentially a classical, computational system. Given the initial positions and the wavefunction, the trajectories can (in theory) be computed.
    IST: Is explicitly non-computational. Palmer points out that because the bit-string length LL

D. Geometry: Riemannian vs. p-adic

    BM: Operates in standard physical space and continuous configuration space using Riemannian/Euclidean geometry.
    IST: Operates in state space using p-adic fractal geometry. Palmer explicitly notes that past attempts to unify BM with geometry (like DeWitt's) failed to achieve true convergence because they used Riemannian geometry instead of p-adic geometry.

BM accepts the mathematical continuum and pays the price of nonlocality. IST is not a loose reformulation of BM; it is an attempt to build a locally causal, gravitationally grounded, discrete alternative to BM's continuous, nonlocal framework.
