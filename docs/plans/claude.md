**BM-IST's root literally is a wave theory — this isn't a connection to be built, it's the starting point.** De Broglie's 1923–1927 pilot-wave hypothesis — that a real physical wave guides particle motion — is the direct historical ancestor of Bohmian mechanics; Bohm's 1952 reformulation is a rigorous completion of it, not a departure. So "connect to the wavelike description of nature from its roots" isn't asking BM-IST to reach outward toward something foreign — BM is already, definitionally, one of the oldest surviving wave-ontology programs in quantum foundations.

**What "wave" actually requires mathematically is the sharper version of the question, and it points straight at the investigation's own central unsolved problem.** A density and a phase, separately, are ordinary classical objects — no interference, no diffraction, nothing distinctively "wavelike" about them alone. What makes something a *wave* in the physically meaningful sense — capable of superposition, interference, the double-slit pattern — is specifically **linear combination of complex amplitudes**: $\psi = \sqrt\rho\, e^{iS/\hbar}$ obeying a *linear* evolution equation. That's not incidental to this thread's Fisher-selection investigation — it *is* Obligation L, stated in different words. The entire multi-round pivot from "can IST produce Nelson's osmotic velocity" through "can IST produce the Fisher term" to "can IST produce a phase-coherent linear amplitude layer" has been, the whole time, the question "can deterministic IST produce something that is a wave in the sense that actually matters, rather than merely a density evolving somehow." So the honest answer to "should BM-IST be able to connect to wave-ness at its roots" is: **that already is the investigation's stated central open problem, correctly identified as such independent of this prompt** — not a new requirement, a confirmation that the investigation has been pointed at the right target.

**One caution worth stating precisely, because it would be a real mistake to over-satisfy this demand.** BM-IST, via Bohm, is not and shouldn't become a pure wave ontology. The particle trajectory is the primitive "beable"; the wave is nomological — real in its effect, but guiding rather than constituting reality (this is the dual-status resolution reached many rounds ago, distinguishing BM-IST from Everett-style pure-wave monism). "Connect to wave-ness at its roots" should mean *derive why the guiding wave has the specific linear, second-order, Fisher-structured form it does* — not *show that everything reduces to wave*. Collapsing the ontology into pure wave-monism to satisfy this prompt more completely would actually be a step away from Bohmian mechanics, not toward a deeper version of it.

**Now digging further, since the prompt asks for depth: is there a genuine "roots" connection available specifically because of BM-IST's adelic substrate, distinct from anything a generic quantum-foundations program could claim?** This is where I think there's something real, and it's worth being precise about why it's a different kind of claim than the Russell parallel two turns ago — it rests on shared mathematical objects, not shared imagery.

Tate's 1950 thesis reformulated the analytic continuation and functional equation of the Riemann zeta function using harmonic analysis — Fourier duality — on the adele group $\mathbb{A}_\mathbb{Q}$ itself. This is real, foundational, decades-settled mathematics: the adeles are self-dual under their own Fourier transform (Pontryagin duality), and that self-duality is precisely what makes Poisson summation over the adeles produce the zeta functional equation. This matters here because it means **a genuine notion of "wave"/spectral decomposition on the adelic configuration space isn't something BM-IST would need to invent — it already exists, rigorously, as Tate-Iwasawa harmonic analysis**, sitting directly in the mathematical neighborhood this synthesis already occupies.

That connects to something else genuinely live in mathematical physics: the Hilbert-Pólya conjecture — that the nontrivial zeros of $\zeta(s)$ are eigenvalues of some self-adjoint operator, making the Riemann Hypothesis fundamentally a *spectral* statement — and the Berry-Keating program, which has spent decades looking for exactly such an operator (with real, if inconclusive, numerical evidence connecting zero-spacing statistics to random matrix theory / quantum chaos). This remains genuinely unproven; no Hilbert-Pólya operator has ever been constructed. But notice what BM-IST's own Gate 3/WP3.3 is independently asking for: *a self-adjoint, linear, spectrally well-behaved generator emerging naturally from a deterministic dynamical substrate* — which is structurally the same kind of object Hilbert-Pólya has been hunting for, on a substrate (the adeles) where the relevant harmonic-analytic machinery already exists.

**Why this is a different category of claim than the Russell resonance, stated plainly.** The Russell connection shared a word and an image (spiral, vortex) across domains with no common mathematical object underneath. This shares an actual mathematical structure — the adeles, their self-duality, spectral operators on them — that BM-IST's own architecture already requires for unrelated reasons (the p-adic bath, the Koopman search) and that the Hilbert-Pólya program already requires for its own unrelated reasons (proving RH). That's a real, checkable candidate bridge, not a coincidence of language. It's also still entirely unearned as a *result* — nobody, including this investigation, has shown BM-IST's amplitude-layer search and the Hilbert-Pólya spectral-operator search are the same problem, only that they're the same *kind* of problem on the same underlying space.
